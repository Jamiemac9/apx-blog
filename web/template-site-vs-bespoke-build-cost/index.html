Template site vs bespoke build: what £550 and £1,997 actually buy a UK small business

The question we get most isn't "can you build my site?" — it's "why is your quote four times the other one?" Usually because one quote is a template deployment and the other is a bespoke build, and nobody told the client which is which. This is the honest breakdown, with our own published prices (£550 and £1,997 are the real figures from our price card, not an invented range) and an admission at the end about when the cheap one is the right one.

First: "template" and "bespoke" are cost categories, not quality tiers

A template site is a pre-built product configured to your brand. A bespoke build is designed and engineered around what your business actually needs. Neither is secretly bad — but they solve different problems, and the difference compounds after launch, not before it.

For scale, SpotDev's 2026 UK pricing guide puts DIY website-builder subscriptions at roughly £100–£300 a year, freelancer brochure sites at £500–£4,000, and small-agency standard sites at £2,500–£10,000 (spotdev.co.uk, accessed 3 September 2026). Our bookable range sits deliberately inside that market: our smaller site project from £550 (£45/month support) and Diamond at £1,997 (£150/month support).

What £550 buys (our smaller site project)

This is the "done properly, not done endlessly" tier. A template deployment in the best sense: a proven structure, your content and imagery placed with intent, the essentials we refuse to skip.

  • Up to a handful of pages from an existing, tested structure — no bespoke layout design
  • Real image optimisation (WebP conversion at delivery sizes, the pipeline we use on every build — measured on a live project here)
  • Mobile-responsive, contact route, basic on-page SEO (titles, meta descriptions), analytics wired in
  • Two revision rounds included, deposit before work, balance on completion, 14-day invoices — same terms at every tier
  • Optional £45/month support covering updates and small content changes

What you are not buying: custom functionality, an information architecture designed around your sales process, or someone owning your performance targets. If your business is "people should be able to find us and ring us", £550 is honest and sufficient.

What £1,997 buys (Diamond)

Diamond is where the build starts from a blank page. The BLOC Architecture rebuild — documented before/after here — is this shape of work: multi-route site, per-scheme project pages with downloadable materials schedules, positioning written around how customers actually search ("Residential Architects UK"), GA4 tracking, and an image system where every asset has a deliberate weight and delivery size.

The maths nobody puts on the quote

A £100–£300/year builder subscription becomes expensive the month you need something it can't do — a per-project page, a lead form that routes intelligently, a Core Web Vitals problem your host won't touch. Template economics are "cheap until it isn't, then it's a rebuild". Bespoke economics are "expensive once, then you're editing a site that already fits the business". A £3,000 quote including twelve months of support routinely beats a £2,000 quote that bills every change — same logic applies one tier down.

When we'd take the £550

Honestly: sole traders with clear content, no funnels, no differentiation problem; businesses validating a brand before investing; anyone whose template site only needs to exist while something else drives enquiries. We'd rather down-sell a Diamond pitch than sell a build that waits for a business to grow into it. And if you're on the fence, the deciding question isn't budget — it's whether your site has to do something (rank for a competitive term, present a portfolio, route leads). "Be findable" is a £550 job. "Sell like the business does" is a design-and-engineer job.
